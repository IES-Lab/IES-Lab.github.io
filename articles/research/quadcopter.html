Quadcopters' control

At the University of Illinois, I developed a model-based nonlinear attitude control to improve a quadcopter's agility, by modifying a nonlinear attitude control that was originally designed for a spacecraft. I also built a monocular-vision position estimator for a quadcopter's localization that is computationally efficient enough to be implemented in real-time.


Model-based attitude controller

A quadrotor’s translation motion is coupled with the quadrotor's attitude and thrust whose dynamics include nonlinear terms of the rotational matrix with respect to the quadrotor's orientation, so attitude control is one of the most important parts in improving the UAV's performance. To improve the quadcopter’s agility, I built a quadrotor testbed and modified a model-based nonlinear attitude control which was originally designed for spacecrafts.

The nonlinear attitude controller was designed by modeling the dynamics of the quadrotor and characterizing the quadrotor’s physical properties. From the 1st Lyapunov method, the attitude controller is globally exponentially stable without perturbation, which gives bounded errors that can be estimated with respect to bounded disturbance or modeling errors.

The stability and agility of the controller are validated by both simulations and experiments and its performance is compared with one of a conventional PID attitude controller. The simulations were implemented by computing the quadcopter dynamics numerically on MATLAB and Simulink. In the experiments, a customized quadcopter of 450mm X-shaped frame is used and the attitude controller is implemented by modifying the PX4 autopilot system.


Monocular-vision position estimator

Due to the observability issue, a quadcopter requires an additional position estimator in addition to IMU/Gyro sensors to localize its position. With this motivation, I proposed a monocular-vision and computationally-efficient position estimator for a quadcopter's localization.

The position estimator detects visual markers of the image and computes the quadcopter's position with respect to the markers by geometric relation. The estimator runs in real-time with Kalman filter to deal with errors and the four circles on the ground are used as visual markers that give geometric information of circles given.

The performance of the position estimator was evaluated independently from a quadcopter control. The estimator was implemented on a Crazyflie nano quadcopter and tested by the quadcopter's hovering.
